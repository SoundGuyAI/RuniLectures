Move 1 - Plan the day

Bogota traffic sets your agenda

Guests are expected to be on time even when counterparts run late. EDC's Bogota office calls three meetings a day unrealistic - plan for two.[1]

Move 2 - Stay close

Business moves to WhatsApp

Face time matters: a senior visit still counts, and representatives alone are not enough.[1] Between visits, WhatsApp is one of the most common ways to stay connected in Colombia.[1]

Have your materials professionally translated into Spanish - EDC cautions against relying on machine translation.[1]

Field guide - Gifts and generations

Gifts, and who is in the room

The gesture matters more than the price.[3]

Gifts are mostly social, not routine business.[3] If invited home: fruit, a plant, or quality chocolates.[3] Roses are fine; lilies and marigolds carry funeral associations.[3] Usually opened later, not on the spot.[3]

Age and seniority shape decisions; titles run the meeting.[3] A 519-worker study in Santander, Colombia found work values differ by cohort - prestige and impact matter less to Gen Z than to Gen X.[9] Cohort labels vary by study and country; some "generational" gaps are age or life-stage effects.[10]

Calibrate live: ask your host how their team splits.
