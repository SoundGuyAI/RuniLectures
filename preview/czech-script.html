Czech Republic. You have three working days in Prague and a supply agreement to open. Here is how trade advisors say to plan it. Three moves, two contrasts, and one rule that travels everywhere: ask, check, adapt.

Book it weeks ahead. Trade guidance says to start arranging meetings several weeks before your visit - impromptu meetings are unwelcome - and to be punctual, even early. First contact goes to a named person, by formal email, and you confirm a few days before. Avoid Friday afternoons, when many Czechs leave early for the country cottage, and the summer and holiday weeks that are harder to book.

Titles first. Open with "Dobry den", a handshake, and pan or pani plus surname and title - then wait to be invited to first names. Guides advise a serious demeanor at first: it reads as respect for you and the deal, not coldness. Dress conservatively at first, and keep small talk to hockey, football or technology - not politics.

No quick-sale push. A barrage of quick meetings and follow-up emails tends to backfire; expect slower, procedural decisions made at senior levels. A business lunch is formal and multi-course, and rushing it is considered ill-mannered. Choose and support a local agent, show after-sales capability, and bring materials in Czech or dual Czech-English, in metric units; do not assume English outside Prague. And expect sharp price sensitivity.
