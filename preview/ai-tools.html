03 - The loop that built this kit

Steps 1-4: brief to critique

1Briefdone
The human writes the goal. One WhatsApp message defined the course, the four countries, the deliverables, the repo and the documentation requirement.
2Researchdone
Sources before sentences: published business-culture guides and official pages for each country, so every claim can carry a source tag.
3Prototypedone
v1 fast, expecting it to be wrong. It was: it told rules instead of teaching judgment.
4Critiquedone
Review is where quality enters. Does it stereotype? Does it pretend there is one right answer? Is every claim sourced? Are gifts and officials handled with compliance care? Does it work on a projector and on a phone?

Steps 5-8: revise to media

5Revisedone
Same loop, better artifact: hypotheses plus verification questions, scenarios with tradeoffs instead of quizzes, claim-level citations, checklists instead of tests.
6Verifydone
Look at the actual pixels: pages rendered and inspected at desktop and phone sizes, interactions exercised in a test browser. A DOM success message is not proof that something looks right.
7Publishin progress
Branch, pull request, human merges. The site ships through PRs the human owner reviews - the agent does not merge its own work.

Pilot first, then scale: one voiceover and one motion video get reviewed before any batch production - the same loop applied to media.

05 - Rules of the loop

Workflow, not gambling

Sources or it didn't happen. AI writes drafts; it also confabulates. Verification is your job, and it is gradable.
Disclose the AI. This kit discloses its AI use on every page. Your assignment requires the same. Disclosure is professionalism, not confession.
Review before scale. Pilot first - one video, one narration, one page, reviewed - then batch. Never mass-produce what you haven't inspected.
The human owns the decision. Budgets, sends and merges stay with the human. Keep it that way in your own projects.
06 - Your turn

Run the same loop for your clip

1Brief - one message: audience, country, three cultural points, tone, length. Steal the structure of the briefs on the previous slide.
2Research - get your cultural claims from credible sources first (the country briefings here list theirs). Paste the sources into your prompt.
3Prototype - generate a first clip with a video tool (for example Google Flow). Expect it to be wrong.
4Critique and revise - list exactly what is off: facts, tone, pacing, visuals. Two or three loops is normal.
5Verify and disclose - check every claim against your sources, watch the final render end to end, and add the AI-use disclosure the assignment requires.
