Field briefing - Colombia

Two meetings a day.
Not five.

A working visit to Bogota, planned from Colombian trade sources - not guesses.[1][2]

Ask - Check - Adapt
Move 1 - Plan the day

Bogota traffic sets your agenda

Guests are expected to be on time even when counterparts run late. EDC's Bogota office calls three meetings a day unrealistic - plan for two.[1]

Move 2 - Stay close

Business moves to WhatsApp

Face time matters: a senior visit still counts, and representatives alone are not enough.[1] Between visits, WhatsApp is one of the most common ways to stay connected in Colombia.[1]

Have your materials professionally translated into Spanish - EDC cautions against relying on machine translation.[1]

Illustrative scenario
Buenas tardes! Ready for the proposal?
Sending the Spanish version tonight, translated properly.
Perfect. Our director joins Friday's call.
We'll bring the after-sales plan - that is what she will ask about.[2]
Move 3 - Expect the negotiation

Prepare for the price conversation.

Know the local pricebefore you quote[1]
Research what your sector actually charges in Colombia - and what competitors offer.[1]
Prepare arguments for your price; payment terms can be your lever.[1]
Plan local after-sales support as part of the offer, not an extra - trade.gov advises demonstrating service capability in-country.[2]

Direct but framed positively; an optimistic tone "can mask underlying criticism". A "yes" is a commitment, praise may not be.[5]

Move: ask what happens next. Praise plus a calendar invite is a stronger signal than praise alone; watch the follow-through.

COLOMBIA
Illustrative scenario

Warmth protects the relationship; meaning is read between the lines.[6] Politeness is not a purchase order.

Saying "no" without saying it

Illustrative scenario

Argument is part of the communication culture; honesty is expected and respected.[4]

Move: don't take offense. Argue back with data.

Illustrative scenario
"We're excited, but the numbers need work."

Direct, outcome-focused, framed as a solution; the criticism is in the second half of the sentence.[5]
