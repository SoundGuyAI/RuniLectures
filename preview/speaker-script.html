1. Meet the speaker

Oded Sharon, M.B.A., is a game developer, XR creator and lecturer. He builds narrative games, mixed-reality experiences and practical AI-agent workflows. His work connects play, storytelling and production: how to turn an idea into a prototype, test it with people, and keep improving it. This short profile shows the path behind the session.

2. The road here

Oded's route runs from Adventure Mob to Corbomite Games. Adventure Mob was the earlier studio for narrative and comedy games. Corbomite Games is the current home for games, XR and experimental interactive work. Along the way came projects such as Bolt Riley, a reggae adventure game, and Oh SHIP!, part of a shipped XR and interactive catalog. Tap a card for more context.

3. Current projects

The current projects keep testing new ways to play. Save Point is a narrative web game about deja vu, repetition and player choice. Meditation: EXTREME turns calm under chaos into comedy. Where Are My Glasses? uses mixed reality to turn an everyday search into a game. And Wall Tones 2: which is a game, but also another type of game inside. Different formats, same question: what new behavior can the play invite?

4. AI is the workflow

AI is now part of the production loop. Oded's CodexTalk session showed nineteen projects moving in parallel: a written brief, agents doing the work, evidence coming back, and a human verifying the result. Wall Tones is one example - a mixed-reality memory game that turns the room into the board. The link on this slide opens the full CodexTalk session. The point is not more output; it is a repeatable way to get from idea to tested work.

5. Beyond the studio

The story continues outside the studio. Awards, Burning Man and improv all feed the same creative practice: make something, put it in front of people, and learn from the reaction. He also once met Captain America. Tap the cards for context. Play, community and stage time are not side notes here; they are part of how the work stays experimental and human.

6. Why this session is hands-on

That is why this session is hands-on. Do not just watch the loop; run it. Start with a brief. Research before claiming. Prototype early. Verify the result. Then publish with human review. The student stays editor-in-chief: choose the goal, inspect the evidence, and make the call.
