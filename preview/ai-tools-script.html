AI Workflow session narration script (draft)

646 spoken words - about 4:18 to 4:58 at a measured pace (130-150 words per minute). This is the exact text the voice will speak. Source references for every claim are on the last slide of the briefing.

The AI Workflow. This session shows the loop behind the kit you just used - the research, copy, design, code and repo were produced by AI agents steered by a human. You are the editor-in-chief. The loop has eight steps: brief, research, prototype, critique, revise, verify, publish, media.

First, the shift: from answers to agents. Then, you asked a chatbot for text, copied it, and did the real work yourself - the AI was a smart typewriter. Now, you describe an outcome; an agent plans, executes across tools, and reports back with evidence. Your job shifts to direction, review and judgment - the skills this course grades. Quality now depends on how well you brief, check and correct.

The toolbox: five tools, five jobs. Coding agents like Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex read a codebase, edit files and work with git - delegate well-scoped coding tasks and review the diffs. Cursor is an AI-first code editor - delegate the implementation, keep your attention on direction and review. Personal assistants like Instinct work where you already talk, researching, drafting and building while checking with you before acting. And Google Flow generates video from text prompts and references. Treat these as options for your own projects; the tool descriptions come from each tool's official page.

The loop that built this kit, steps one to four. Brief: the human writes the goal - one WhatsApp message defined the course, the countries, the deliverables and the repo. Research: sources before sentences, so every claim can carry a source tag. Prototype: version one fast, expecting it to be wrong - and it was; it told rules instead of teaching judgment. Critique: review is where quality enters - does it stereotype, does it pretend there is one right answer, is every claim sourced?

Steps five to eight. Revise: same loop, better artifact - hypotheses plus verification questions, scenarios with tradeoffs, claim-level citations. Verify: look at the actual pixels - pages rendered and inspected at desktop and phone sizes; a success message is not proof that something looks right. Publish: branch, pull request, human merges - the agent does not merge its own work. Media: pilot first, then scale - one voiceover and one motion video get reviewed before any batch production.

The actual prompts, typos included. "Make all the presentation interactive sites - fun and juicy and flashing and interactive, and not just words or slides." One sentence set the design bar. The lesson: describe the experience you want, not the implementation. And the meta-prompt - "document all my prompts and the workflow so the students will know how I use AI agents" - is why this session exists. You can ask for the process itself to be documented.

The rules of the loop. Sources or it didn't happen: AI writes drafts; it also confabulates - verification is your job. Disclose the AI: this kit discloses its AI use on every page, and your assignment requires the same - disclosure is professionalism, not confession. Review before scale: never mass-produce what you haven't inspected. And the human owns the decision: budgets, sends and merges stay with the human.

Your turn: run the same loop for your own clip. Brief it in one message - audience, country, three cultural points, tone, length. Research your claims from credible sources first, and paste the sources into your prompt. Prototype with a video tool and expect it to be wrong. Critique and revise - two or three loops is normal. Then verify every claim against your sources, watch the final render end to end, and disclose the AI.

Where this comes from: the tool descriptions come from each tool's official page, with no pricing or version claims, because those change. This session itself was researched, written and built by AI agents steered by a human, and reviewed on real renders. That is the loop, applied to itself.
