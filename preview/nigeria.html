Field briefing - Nigeria

Greetings first.
Then business.

A working visit to Lagos, planned from verified trade and practitioner sources - not guesses.[1][2]

Ask - Check - Adapt
Move 1 - Plan the ground game

Lagos for deals, Abuja for government

Lagos is the commercial hub; Federal Government meetings sit in Abuja.[1][3] Wherever you meet: appointments are sometimes postponed at short notice, so confirm, reconfirm, and call while you are on the way - embassy guidance calls this normal.[3] You are still expected to arrive on time yourself.[2]

T-1 DAY
Reconfirm the meeting[3]
EN ROUTE
Call the office or assistant while travelling[3]
ON SITE
Arrive on time - keep your own timekeeping standard[2]
IF MOVED
Short-notice postponements happen; rebook without complaint[3]
Move 2 - Greetings and titles

How was the night?

Greetings come first and can be lengthy - health, work, family - before any agenda; jumping straight to business reads as rude.[5] Use honorific and professional titles in full unless invited otherwise, and greet the most senior person with seniority in mind.[1][2] Give and receive with the right hand.[4]

Illustrative scenario
Good morning, Chief Adeyemi. How was the night?
The night was good, thank you. And your family?
All well, thank you for asking.
Well done. Now - let us hear your proposal.
Move 3 - Earning the yes

Bargaining is the relationship

Significant deals close face-to-face, over repeat visits - not quickly or impersonally.[1]
Once the relationship warms, much of the work moves to WhatsApp.[1]
Expect to be challenged on price in the room - bargaining is an expected part of the process, and a discount is a relationship move ("making you my customer").[2]
A warm, agreeable answer is not a commitment - test it by what survives follow-up.[2]
Dress signals seriousness: a well-fitted dark suit or formal national dress; overly casual dress can read as a casual attitude.[1][2]
Plan instalment-based offers where upfront payment is hard; foreign-exchange access is limited.[8]
Contrast - Same moment, three rooms

Giving critical feedback

ISRAEL
Illustrative scenario
"This will not work. Here is why."

Straightforward and assertive style; the municipality guide frames it as honesty in service of the business relationship.[6]

Move: hear the content, not the volume. Answer with data.

UNITED STATES
Illustrative scenario
"Overall it was excellent - a few small tweaks to this part."

Americans often give several positives for every negative, and the negative can be the key point.[7]

Move: same check as in every room - ask exactly what needs to change, and watch the follow-through.

NIGERIA
Illustrative scenario
"It is well. We will look at it."

One practitioner guide (2015, treat as dated) describes no feedback-giving culture: indirect, diplomatic feedback is accepted but not expected - and juniors may not openly disagree with seniors.[2]

Move: ask open questions; make it easy to raise problems privately.

Contrast - Interruptions

When the meeting stops mid-sentence

ISRAEL
Illustrative scenario
(Three people finish your sentence for you.)

Colleagues may interrupt out of enthusiasm to relate and share, not lack of interest.[6]

Move: overlap back; it is participation, not disrespect.
