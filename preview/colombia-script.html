Colombia narration script

About 2.5 minutes at a measured pace. Source references for every claim are on the last slide of the briefing.

Colombia. You have one working day in Bogota, two meetings that matter, and a proposal to land. Here is how experienced trade advisors say to plan it. Three moves, two contrasts, and one rule that travels everywhere: ask, check, adapt.

Plan two meetings a day, not five. EDC's Bogota office calls three a day unrealistic in city traffic, so allow generous travel time between them. Guests are expected on time, even when counterparts run late. And keep the late afternoon open: the advice is a light schedule, not a packed one.

Face time matters. EDC's advice: a senior visit still counts, and sending representatives alone is not enough. Between visits, WhatsApp is one of the most common ways to stay connected in Colombia. And when you send material, have it professionally translated into Spanish; EDC cautions against relying on machine translation.

Prepare for the price conversation. Know what your sector actually charges in Colombia before you quote, and prepare the arguments for your price; payment terms can be your lever. And plan local after-sales support as part of the offer: the trade.gov guide advises showing buyers you can service what you sell in-country.

Same moment, three rooms, three illustrative scenarios. In Israel, expect a direct, frank style, where contradicting opinions are spoken openly. In the US, an optimistic "this is great stuff" can sometimes mask criticism, so ask what happens next. In Colombia, warmth protects the relationship, and politeness is not a purchase order. Different packaging, same discipline in all three: praise is a signal, not a commitment. Ask for the concrete next step, and watch the follow-through.

Hearing no, in three more illustrative scenarios. An Israeli counterpart might say the price is not serious, straight out; argument is part of the communication culture. An American counterpart might wrap it in excitement; the "but" is the message. A Colombian counterpart might protect the relationship with "perhaps we can review the details next week"; disagreement is often handled indirectly to protect dignity. These sample lines are teaching scenarios, not documented quotes. Your move in each room: protect the relationship, and ask exactly what needs to change.

Everything you just heard comes from named business and culture sources, on screen now. The illustrations are AI-generated and labeled. Customs vary by region, sector and company, so before you act on any of this: ask, check, adapt.
