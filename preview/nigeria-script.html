Nigeria narration script (draft)

Nigeria. You have one week, split between Lagos and Abuja, and a distribution deal to earn. Here is how trade and practitioner sources say to work it. Three moves, two contrasts, and one rule that travels everywhere: ask, check, adapt.

Plan the ground game. Lagos is the commercial hub; Federal Government meetings sit in Abuja. Wherever you meet: confirm, reconfirm, and call while you are on the way. Embassy guidance is blunt - appointments are sometimes postponed at short notice, and that is normal. You are still expected to arrive on time yourself, so allow for changes, and reconfirm.

Greetings first. Then business. A culture guide warns that greetings can be lengthy - health, work, family - before any agenda, and that jumping straight to business reads as rude; it labels these general tendencies, not rules. Practitioner guides advise using honorific and professional titles in full unless invited otherwise, and greeting with seniority in mind.

Bargaining is the relationship. Significant deals close face-to-face, over repeat visits - not quickly, and not impersonally. Once the relationship warms, trade guidance describes much of the work moving to WhatsApp. A British Council business guide advises: expect to be challenged on price in the room - a discount is a relationship move, "making you my customer". A warm, agreeable answer is not a commitment: test it by what survives follow-up. Guides also note that dress signals seriousness. And trade guidance reports foreign-exchange access is limited; where upfront payment is hard, pay-as-you-go instalment offers are a working pattern.

Same moment, three rooms, three illustrative scenarios. In Israel, feedback is straightforward - honesty in service of the business relationship. In the US, expect several positives for every negative, and the negative can be the key point. In Nigeria, one practitioner guide from 2015 - treat it as dated - describes indirect, diplomatic feedback: accepted, but not expected, and juniors may not openly disagree with seniors. Everywhere: ask open questions, and make it easy to raise problems privately.

Interruptions, in three more rooms. In Israel, colleagues may jump in mid-sentence - one guide says this reflects enthusiasm, not lack of interest; overlap can reflect engagement, so watch your counterpart and adjust. For the US, our source register has no guidance on interruptions, so we make no claim - ask and observe. In Nigeria, your host may take calls or receive a visitor mid-pitch; practitioner advice is patience - pause graciously, and resume where you left off. These are teaching scenarios, not documented quotes.

Everything you just heard comes from named trade and practitioner sources, on screen now, with their dates and qualifiers. The illustrations are AI-generated and labeled. Customs vary by region, sector and company, so before you act on any of this: ask, check, adapt.
