Course practice

Prompt examples

Adapt these examples to your assignment. They are teaching templates, not a transcript of anyone's messages or a record of how this site was built.

1. Define the learning outcome

Create an interactive briefing for business students preparing to meet a delegation from [country]. Cover meeting preparation, communication, presenting and gifts. Use short visual slides, practical scenarios and named sources.

Specify the audience, learning goal and format. Keep personal details out of the prompt.

2. Demand specific evidence

Replace generic advice with country-specific claims. Cite a named source for each claim. Distinguish evidence from design suggestions, identify dated guidance and flag what remains uncertain.

A plausible answer is a draft. Open the sources and check that they support the claim.

3. Practice judgment

Turn this guidance into a meeting scenario. Offer two plausible responses, explain the tradeoffs and suggest a question that would help clarify the counterpart's expectations. Label invented dialogue as illustrative.

Teach how to ask and adapt, rather than presenting national stereotypes as rules.

4. Make the briefing usable

Add a concise do-and-don't summary. Give every item a source. Keep visible wording short, put detail behind a click and check readability on a phone and a projected screen.

Review the actual presentation, including interactions and small-screen layout.

5. Compare with a human source

Compare this briefing with the course material provided for the assignment. Separate what is confirmed, what is missing and what has been oversimplified. Preserve qualifiers about region, generation, sector and company.

Only supply material you are allowed to share. A local source may change the draft.

6. Verify the final media

Check that the narration matches the current slide order and wording. Label generated images and fictional examples. List the references and disclose AI use. Flag any claim that still needs checking.

Watch and listen before submitting. Correct the work rather than trusting the completion message.

For real-world use, check the recipient's policies and preferences before acting. Customs vary within countries. Ask, check, adapt.
