The AI Workflow

The tools, and more importantly the loop, behind this entire kit. Everything on this site - research, copy, design, code, repo - was produced by AI agents steered by a human. This session shows you exactly how, so you can do it for your assignment.

01The shift: from answers to agents

The first wave of AI tools answered questions. The current wave does work: reads your files, writes code, runs it, checks the result, and iterates - while you steer.

Then: ask and copy

You asked a chatbot for text, copied it, and did the real work yourself. The AI was a smart typewriter.

Now: delegate and review

You describe an outcome. An agent plans, executes across tools, and reports back with evidence. Your job shifts to direction, review and judgment - the skills this course is grading.

The one sentence to remember: you are not the typist anymore; you are the editor-in-chief. Quality now depends on how well you brief, check and correct.

03The loop that built this kit

The real skill is not any single tool - it is the loop. This is the sequence this kit went through, with completed steps and in-progress steps marked honestly.

1. Brief done - the human writes the goal

One WhatsApp message defined the course, the four countries, the deliverables (interactive sites, voiceovers, motion videos), the repo, and the documentation requirement.

2. Research done - sources before sentences

Research came first: published business-culture guides and official pages for each country, so claims on the country pages can carry source tags.

3. Prototype done - v1 fast, expect it to be wrong

A first interactive prototype, built quickly - and on review it was not good enough: it told rules instead of teaching judgment.

4. Critique done - review is where quality enters

The review questions to run on any AI draft: Does it stereotype? Does it pretend there is one right answer? Is every claim sourced? Are gifts and officials handled with compliance care? Does it work on a projector and on a phone?

5. Revise done - same loop, better artifact

The second version rebuilt the framing: hypotheses plus verification questions, scenarios with tradeoffs instead of quizzes, claim-level citations, checklists instead of tests.

6. Verify done - look at the actual pixels

Pages get rendered and inspected at desktop and phone sizes, and interactions get exercised in a test browser - DOM success messages are not proof that something looks right.

7. Publish in progress - branch, PR, human merges

The site lives on a branch with an open pull request; preview hosting is being set up. The human owner reviews and merges - the agent does not merge its own work.

8. Media next - pilot first, then scale

One voiceover script and one motion-video pilot get reviewed before any batch production - the same loop applied to media.

05Rules of the loop

What separates a professional AI workflow from gambling with a chatbot.

Sources or it didn't happen

Every factual claim in this kit links to a published source. AI writes drafts; it also confabulates. Verification is your job, and it is gradable.

Disclose the AI

This kit discloses its AI use on every page. Disclosure is professionalism, not confession.

Review before scale

Pilot first: one video, one narration, one page - reviewed - then batch. Never mass-produce what you haven't inspected.

The human owns the decision

Branches and pull requests exist so a person approves what ships. Budgets, sends and merges stay with the human. Keep it that way in your own projects.

06Your turn

Assignment #1 asks for an AI video clip explaining your country's business-culture approach, with AI-use disclosure. Run the same loop you just saw:

1

Brief: write your goal in one message - audience, country, three cultural points, tone, length. Steal the structure of the briefs on this page.

2

Research: get your cultural claims from credible sources first (the country briefings here list theirs). Paste sources into your prompt.

3

Prototype: generate a first clip with a video tool (for example Google Flow). Expect it to be wrong.

4

Critique and revise: list exactly what is off - facts, tone, pacing, visuals - and iterate. Two or three loops is normal.

5

Verify and disclose: check every claim against your sources, watch the final render end to end, and add the AI-use disclosure required by the assignment.
