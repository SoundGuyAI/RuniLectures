Field briefing

Nigeria

Africa's most populous country and one of its biggest entrepreneurial engines. Business runs on relationships, respect and reading the room. Starting hypotheses and the questions that verify them - not a rulebook.

Relationship before transaction Titles and greetings matter Ask, check, adapt

01Relationship is the market

Guides describe Nigerian business culture as strongly relationship-first: people want to know who they are dealing with before they discuss terms.[1] That is not preamble to skip - it is the due diligence.

Small talk is the real meeting

Expect extended personal conversation before business: family, health, football, how you are finding Nigeria.[1] Working move: budget the time and engage genuinely. Rushing to the deck signals you are not invested in the relationship.

Not one Nigeria

Lagos, Abuja, Kano and Port Harcourt run differently; guides note real regional and religious diversity (a largely Muslim north and largely Christian south, hundreds of ethnic groups).[1] Working move: ask where your counterparts work and live, and calibrate to that city and community - not to "Nigeria".

Working hypothesis: fast, commercial, high-energy; Nigeria's business capital rewards hustle and punishes vague follow-through.[1] Check by: confirming next steps in writing the same day - energy is high and calendars move fast.

Working hypothesis: the political capital runs more formal and protocol-driven, especially near government.[1] Check by: asking about agenda, titles and seating expectations in advance of official meetings.

Working hypothesis: more conservative norms: modest dress, religious rhythms (Friday prayers), and no assumption that alcohol is part of hospitality.[1] Check by: asking your host about dress and scheduling around prayer times.

Greetings carry real weight - this is where respect is established.[1]

03Cards, clothes, gifts

Small details, checked in advance.

Business cards

  • Exchange is common; give and receive with the right hand[1]
  • Include your title and qualifications - credentials are read[1]

Dress

  • Smart business dress signals seriousness; Lagos corporate rooms run formal[1]
  • Modest dress in the north; lightweight fabrics in the heat[1]
  • Well-dressed is a compliment to your host - ask ahead about the room

Gifts between companies

  • Gifts are described as appreciated but not expected in business; modest and thoughtful beats expensive[1]
  • Avoid anything that could read as leverage - see the compliance note below

Compliance first

Nigeria has active anti-corruption enforcement and many officials are bound by gift and ethics rules. Keep any gift modest, transparent and clearly ceremonial - and for officials, confirm the plan with the teaching team before the visit. Never improvise additional personal gifts for an official.

04Anatomy of a Nigerian deal

A composite from the guides - a shape to expect, not a script to impose.[1]

The introduction who vouches for you

Warm introductions carry real weight; networks and referrals open doors that cold outreach does not.[1]

Israeli instinct to manage: pure cold-email hustle. In Nigeria, one trusted mutual contact is worth fifty clever subject lines.

The greetings unhurried, personal

Titles, eldest first, real small talk.[1]

Israeli instinct to manage: "let's skip the pleasantries, we have 30 minutes." Here the pleasantries are the point.

The pitch story + substance

Energy and vision land well, but expect detailed questions on price, terms and delivery; decisions rest with the senior person.[1]

Israeli instinct to manage: negotiating with whoever is in the room. Ask early: "who else should be part of this conversation?"

The answer listen between the lines

Indirect politeness means hesitation may arrive as "we will discuss it" or warm words without dates.[1]

Israeli instinct to manage: reading enthusiasm as agreement. Confirm with specifics: "what happens next, and when?"

The close paper and patience

Put terms in writing; expect some negotiation - bargaining is a normal, social part of business.[1]

Israeli instinct to manage: treating negotiation as conflict. Here it is relationship - enjoy it, and keep it warm.

06Calibrate: instincts you bring from Israel

Israeli business culture is documented as informal, fast and very direct.[2][3] Nigerian culture is documented as relationship-first, title-conscious and politely indirect.[1] Both work. Know which room you are in.

Your default

Dugri: say it straight, disagreement is honest and respected.

CHECK
Possible read in Lagos

Blunt disagreement can cost the relationship the deal depends on.[1] Keep the honesty, soften the delivery: questions before corrections.

Your default

First names instantly; titles feel stiff and old-fashioned.

CHECK
Possible read in Lagos

Titles are earned status and using them is respect, not stiffness.[1] Use them until invited otherwise.

Your default

Get to business fast; relationship grows through working together.

CHECK
Possible read in Lagos

The relationship is the precondition for the work, not its byproduct.[1] Invest in it first and visibly.

07The thank-you ceremony

Same 2-3 minute course ceremony, tuned for a Nigerian diplomat. The ceremony gift is coordinated through the course - confirm with the teaching team what it is and who presents it. Your graded work is the preparation and the words.

1

Full titles, warm delivery. Address the diplomat with their complete title - Your Excellency where applicable - and mean the warmth. Respect shown precisely is remembered.[1]

2

Reference something specific and human from their talk - a story they told, a value they stressed. Personal recognition lands harder than protocol here.

3

Energy is welcome. Unlike more reserved cultures, visible enthusiasm and a smile fit the room. Big gratitude, sincerely expressed, is on-brand.

4

Connect the gift to both cultures with a real fact - Nigerian literature, music or enterprise meeting Israeli innovation. If you reference Nollywood or Afrobeat, know what you are talking about.

5

Present and receive with the right hand, ask before photos, follow up warmly in writing.[1]

Compliance note: diplomats and public officials are often covered by gift and ethics policies. Confirm the gift plan with the teaching team, and never improvise additional personal gifts for an official.
